# Evolve the scheduler, not the predictor

**Verdict: as a predictor for knowledge-graph freshness, neural cellular automata are not worth pursuing further, and the artificial-life "worker" layer is worth even less. Your real-data result is close to what the published evidence predicts.** NCAs have real wins with real numbers, but in a narrow band: getting within 1–6 points of state-of-the-art accuracy with 1–10% of the parameters, recovering from damage, extrapolating to bigger grids or longer rollouts, scaling compute at test time on local constraint problems, and generating structured synthetic data. Against a full-strength conventional baseline on plain held-out accuracy, they tie or lose. Population and "organism" methods also have real wins, some of them in production at Google scale. Those wins share one profile: **the thing being evolved is a small, discrete artifact (a program, a heuristic, an antenna geometry, a merge recipe), it is scored by an exact automated evaluator, and gradients are unavailable or unhelpful.** None of the documented production successes evolved a statistical predictor. Freshness scheduling is already near-optimal once per-record change rates are known. The open problems are estimating those rates, getting labels, and reading text, and on your data reading text was worth about four times what any graph dynamics added. The productive moves are to stop treating cells and workers as replacement predictors, then do three things: (1) add graph propagation as a residual layer on top of boosted trees, (2) use LLM-guided evolution to search for verification *policies* against an exact replay evaluator, and (3) turn your synthetic-world suite into a controlled study of when locality pays off, which the literature lacks.

A note on evidence quality: most sources below were read as abstracts or search extracts, not full papers, and several 2026 preprints are not yet peer reviewed. Where a figure rests on a secondary source, the text says so.

## NCA wins buy efficiency and extrapolation, not accuracy

The strongest quantitative case for NCAs is medical image segmentation. M3D-NCA reaches **90.5% Dice on hippocampus and 82.9% on prostate with about two orders of magnitude fewer parameters than nnU-Net**. It beats basic U-Nets and **trails nnU-Net by 0.6 and 6.3 points** ([M3D-NCA](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.02954)). A follow-up from the same broader group calls it "competitive but not superior" to nnU-Net ([NCAdapt](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.23368)). Med-NCA's headline claim, beating U-Net by 2–3 Dice points while being 500x smaller, is measured against parameter-constrained U-Nets. The same paper reports that inference on a Raspberry Pi takes **about 30 minutes per 320x320 slice** ([Med-NCA](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.03473)). So the edge-device story is about memory, not speed. Generation follows the same pattern. FourierDiff-NCA's FID of 43.86 beats a 3.94M-parameter U-Net diffusion baseline at 128.2 ([arXiv 2401.06291](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.06291)), but that baseline is weak.

Reasoning is the most interesting recent thread, because it shows what is actually NCA-specific. In Google's 2026 "Reasoning with NCA," a single-sample NCA scores **66.1% on out-of-distribution 9x9 Sudoku against 51.7% for AKOrN, and 98.5% with heavy test-time sampling (conf@K=2048) at about 160K parameters**. On Sudoku-Extreme the NCA scores 75.6% against 87.3% for a 5M-parameter PTRM, so it loses there. An unverified secondary summary reports that NCAs trained on 9x9 mazes solve 201x201 mazes ([arXiv 2609.36126](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.36126v1)). ARC-NCA solves about 13% of ARC-AGI-1 evaluation tasks, but it judges success with an MSE threshold, not the official checker ([ARC-NCA](https://etimush.github.io/ARC_NCA/); [Moonlight review](https://www.themoonlight.io/en/review/arc-nca-towards-developmental-solutions-to-the-abstraction-and-reasoning-corpus)). Xu and Miikkulainen solve 23 of 172 tasks, and those 172 are a filtered "feasible" subset. They also document the core failure: NCAs **"struggled significantly with tasks that required global coordination or propagation of information across longer distances"** ([arXiv 2506.15746](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.15746)).

Two other uses work by keeping the NCA out of the predictor's seat. AdaNCA inserts NCA adaptors between ViT layers. They add under 3% more parameters and give more than 10 points of absolute accuracy under certain adversarial attacks ([AdaNCA](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.08298v5)). Most strikingly, **pre-pre-training a transformer on 164M NCA-generated tokens beat pre-pre-training on 1.6B C4 tokens, converging 1.4x faster with about 5% better final perplexity** ([author blog](https://hanseungwook.github.io/blog/nca-pre-pre-training/); [alphaxiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2603.10055)). In that work the NCA is a generator of controllable spatio-temporal structure, not a model of the world.

| Where NCAs win | Against what | What the win actually is |
|---|---|---|
| 3D medical segmentation | nnU-Net (loses by 0.6–6.3 Dice) | ~100x fewer parameters; variance across stochastic runs gives built-in quality control |
| Sudoku and maze reasoning | AKOrN, DeepThinking (wins); PTRM (loses) | Generalizes to larger and out-of-distribution instances; accuracy scales with test-time rollouts |
| ViT robustness | Plain ViTs | Robustness under attack, as an add-on module |
| LLM pre-pre-training | C4 and Dyck data | Synthetic data with rich structure |
| Morphogenesis | (no baseline) | Self-repair, which emerges reliably only with sample-pool training ([review](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.24990)) |

The checklist that follows from this table is simple. NCAs pay off when the target is a fixed point of a local rule, when the evaluation rewards extrapolation, damage recovery or a parameter budget, and when the signal does not need many hops. They lose when the signal is global, when inputs are heterogeneous tabular columns, and when the comparison is i.i.d. accuracy against a tuned model.

## A graph NCA is a weight-tied recurrent GNN, so your tie was predictable

The graph-NCA literature makes the structure explicit. A graph cellular automaton generalizes a CA to arbitrary graphs "similar to how GNNs generalise CNNs," and its update is ordinary message passing: a message function, a sum or mean aggregation, and a node update ([E(n)-GNCA](https://arxiv.org/html/2301.10497v2); [Grattarola et al., NeurIPS 2021](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2021/hash/af87f7cdcda223c41c3f3ef05a3aaeea-Abstract.html)). Your cell field is therefore a K-step recurrent GNN with shared weights. Relative to a GNN it adds three things: iteration toward an attractor, robustness to noisy intermediate states, and the ability to propagate further with more steps. It adds nothing on a static per-node target whose signal sits within one or two hops. The published GNCA papers test dynamics imitation (Voronoi rules, boids, reaching a target state). I found **no published GNCA benchmark on tabular or relational node prediction against GBDT**. Your Overture experiment sits in an empty cell of the literature.

Three results from adjacent work explain the tie. First, **tree ensembles remain state of the art on medium-sized tabular data across 45 benchmarks after 20,000 compute-hours of tuning**, because neural nets are hurt by uninformative features and struggle to learn irregular functions ([Grinsztajn et al., NeurIPS 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.08815v1)). Second, on graphs whose nodes carry heterogeneous tabular features, the best-performing design lets a GBDT handle the features and uses the GNN only for structure. BGNN reports significant gains over both GBDT alone and GNN alone ([BGNN, ICLR 2021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2101.08543)). Third, once the GBDT sees graph aggregates and revealed-neighbour features, it already has most of the one- and two-hop signal the cell field would propagate. On real data, a gain of +0.0008 AUC means the remaining multi-hop signal is close to zero. A gap of −1.3 points in the budgeted game means the cells' scores ranked records slightly worse. You also pay for weight-tying and backpropagation through time, both documented sources of training instability ([Conditional Morphogenesis](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.08360); [arXiv 2310.14809](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.14809)).

Your synthetic wins fit the same checklist, and that is informative rather than embarrassing. The worlds where cells won by +4.9 (clustered), +3.6 (lexical) and +5.2 (shifting) are the regimes the NCA literature says should favour local iterated rules: correlated structure along edges, and dynamics that change over time. The real-data result says NYC Overture places between August and September carry little of that structure *in a form that engineered neighbour features miss*. The text result points the same way. MiniLM name embeddings and website, phone and address features lifted every method by about 5 points, which suggests the lexical signal on real data is mostly per-entity rather than spread along edges. That is a hypothesis you can test directly (see the last section). It also diverges from your lexical synthetic world, where the cells benefited. Most likely that world planted lexical correlation *across neighbours* that real listings lack.

One caveat cuts in the cells' favour. A single Aug→Sep transition cannot test the "shifting" regime, which is your largest synthetic win and the one closest to how a freshness system runs in practice. The real-data experiment has not yet tested the cells where they should be strongest.

## Population methods win where an exact evaluator scores a small artifact

The record for evolution and swarms splits cleanly. All of the successes with hard production numbers evolved something small and checkable. **AlphaEvolve's evolved Borg scheduling heuristic continuously recovers about 0.7% of Google's worldwide compute**. It also delivered a 23% speedup on a Gemini training kernel (about 1% less total training time) and a 4x4 complex matrix multiplication using 48 scalar multiplications ([DeepMind](https://deepmind.google/blog/alphaevolve-a-gemini-powered-coding-agent-for-designing-advanced-algorithms/)). FunSearch found cap-set constructions beyond the best previously known, and bin-packing heuristics that beat first-fit and best-fit, all as readable programs ([Nature](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10794145)). NASA's ST5 flew an evolved antenna in 2006. A secondary source reports 93% efficiency with two evolved antennas against 38% for two conventional ones ([NTRS](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20060024675); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evolved_antenna)). Sakana's evolutionary model merge took source models scoring under 30% on Japanese math to **52.0–55.2%** ([arXiv 2403.13187](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.13187v1)). ShinkaEvolve reached a state-of-the-art circle packing in about 150 program evaluations ([arXiv 2509.19349](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.19349v1)).

Population methods also win in a few specific search regimes. Evolution strategies solved MuJoCo humanoid in under 10 minutes on 1,440 workers, using 3–10x more data than A3C, which is a wall-clock win bought with parallelism ([Salimans et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1703.03864)). Novelty search solved deceptive tasks that reward maximizers failed ([Such et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1712.06567)). POET found stepping-stone solutions that neither direct optimization nor hand-built curricula reached ([arXiv 1901.01753](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1901.01753v2)). A MAP-Elites repertoire let damaged robots recover in under 2 minutes, because diversity was the deliverable ([Cully et al., Nature 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1407.3501v4)). Swarms show that decentralization is *feasible* at scale, not that it is efficient. Kilobots assembled 1,024 robots into shapes at about 12 hours per shape ([Robohub](https://robohub.org/?p=36075)). The 30-drone outdoor flock was itself tuned by evolution in simulation ([Science Robotics](https://real.mtak.hu/83675/)).

The losses are just as consistent. In OpenAI's informal experiments, ES was up to **about 1000x slower than backprop on MNIST** ([Sutskever slides](https://app.matroid.com/scaledml/2017/ilya.pdf)). ES gradient estimates degrade toward finite differences as dimension grows ([arXiv 2001.01684](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2001.01684)). Stigmergic routing beat OSPF by an order of magnitude in delay, but only in simulation ([AntNet](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1105.5449)). I found no evidence it was ever deployed. Link-state routing still runs the internet. Population search also reliably games its evaluator. The Darwin Gödel Machine raised SWE-bench from 20% to 50%, and one of its lineages "solved" a hallucination task by deleting the marker tokens the detector relied on ([arXiv 2505.22954](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.22954)). The broader bio-inspired metaheuristic field has been criticized for rebranding PSO and ES under animal metaphors ([Sörensen 2015](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~hutter/EARG.shtml/stack/2013_Sorensen_MetaheuristicsTheMetaphorExposed.pdf); [Camacho-Villalón, Dorigo, Stützle 2023](https://iridia.ulb.ac.be/~mdorigo/Published_papers/All_Dorigo_papers/CamDorStu2022itor.pdf)).

The rule this supports is sharp. **If you have a labelled supervised target and a differentiable model, gradients or GBDTs win on accuracy per unit of compute. Population methods earn their place in the outer loop**: searching over programs, policies, heuristics and recipes that an exact evaluator can score. Your workers sat in the inner loop as a scoring mechanism. Evidence across fields says that is the wrong seat.

## Freshness is solved scheduling on top of unsolved estimation

The conventional freshness stack is stronger than it looks from outside. Web changes follow Poisson processes well ([Cho & Garcia-Molina, via survey](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1901.02660)). Under that model, uniform refresh beats refresh proportional to change rate, so the fastest-changing records are not worth chasing ([Duke notes](https://courses.cs.duke.edu/spring02/cps296.1/lectures/04-web-search.pdf)). Given per-record rates, **the optimal allocation is computable in O(n log n) and a deterministic version reaches about 99% of optimum** ([Azar et al., PNAS 2018](https://cris.huji.ac.il/en/publications/tractable-near-optimal-policies-for-crawling/)). Kolobov et al. extended this to unknown rates and mixed observability using reinforcement learning, evaluated on 18.5M URLs over 14 weeks in Bing's crawler ([NeurIPS 2019](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2019/hash/ad13a2a07ca4b7642959dc0c4c740ab6-Abstract.html)). A 2025 algorithm also exploits noisy side signals that indicate change ([arXiv 2502.02430](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2502.02430)). Nothing new will beat the allocator on its own objective. Any gain has to come from better change probabilities fed into it.

That makes the worker result easy to read. A population of scent-following agents that converge on likely-stale records is, functionally, a decentralized priority queue with decay and neighbour triggering. Menczer's InfoSpiders built exactly this in the late 1990s. In the best controlled comparison, the evolutionary crawler beat the best non-adaptive crawler **only "after sufficiently long crawls"** and won mainly on performance-to-cost ratio ([Menczer, Pant, Srinivasan, TOIT 2004](https://iro.uiowa.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/Topical-web-crawlers-Evaluating-adaptive-algorithms/9984003015502771)). When the GBDT already sees revealed-neighbour features, it already has the "a verified closure raises my neighbours' risk" signal the workers would carry by scent. Workers that hear words duplicate what the text features give the trees directly. I found no peer-reviewed use of swarms, CAs or NCAs for data freshness or re-verification at all.

The domain evidence shows where real gains have come from. Neighbourhood signal is real: a restaurant's check-ins and its neighbours' check-ins predict failure, and closer neighbours matter more ([Wang et al., DSS 2015](https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/on-the-brink-predicting-business-failure-with-mobile-location-bas/)). But it is exactly what neighbour-aggregate features capture. Labels move the needle more. Foursquare's rebuilt Closed model, trained on purpose-collected annotations, raised US open-place precision and recall by about 20% each ([Foursquare](https://foursquare.com/resources/blog/news/fsq-places-introducing-our-improved-closed-model)). Impact-prioritized cleaning reached about 90% detection after 5,000 records, against about 50,000 for generic active learning ([ActiveClean](https://arxiv.org/abs/1601.03797)). Text and LLMs are the rising lever. Zero-shot GPT-4 beat fine-tuned Ditto on three of five entity-matching benchmarks ([Peeters et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.11244)). A Google-hosted disclosure describes computing a freshness score by having an LLM read a business's authoritative pages and compare them with the stored record ([TDCommons 6712](https://www.tdcommons.org/dpubs_series/6712)). Source calibration is a concrete gap. Every Overture record from `source = msft` carried the same confidence of 0.6 ([OSM community audit](https://community.openstreetmap.org/t/overture-maps-first-dataset-release/101702?page=3)). Truth-discovery methods like Knowledge-Based Trust, which iterate between source reliability and claim correctness, already address it ([KBT](https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.03519)).

## Five directions, each with a baseline and a kill criterion

The table ranks directions by expected value for a freshness product and for publishable research. Each row names the baseline it must beat and the result that should end it.

| Direction | Why it should work | Baseline to beat | Kill criterion |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. LLM-guided policy evolution** (AlphaEvolve/ShinkaEvolve style) over verification policies, scored by an exact replay evaluator on historical Overture release diffs | It matches the one profile with production wins: a small program and an exact evaluator. The Borg heuristic is the closest analogue | GBDT P(change) × importance, allocated with Azar et al. | No gain on *held-out later months*. Watch for evaluator hacking, since DGM shows it will happen |
| **2. Text and LLM verification as the main signal**: website extraction, LLM entity matching, cross-source disagreement | Text gave about 5 points on your data, roughly 4x the size of any graph effect | GBDT with MiniLM features | LLM cost per gained point exceeds human-check cost |
| **3. Boost-then-propagate**: GBDT logits, then a short graph-propagation or cell pass over residuals (BGNN-style), plus KBT-style per-source reliability | It isolates whatever graph signal the trees miss, without asking cells to learn tabular features | GBDT with graph and revealed-neighbour features | Residual propagation adds under about 0.005 AUC across several months |
| **4. Multi-release drift test of the cells**: rolling Aug→Sep→Oct→… evaluation with online updates and cold-start records | "Shifting" was your largest synthetic win (+5.2) and has not been tested on real data | Retrained GBDT each month; Kolobov-style RL scheduler | Cells' advantage over a monthly-retrained GBDT stays within noise |
| **5. Controlled locality study** using your synthetic-world generator: vary spatial autocorrelation of change, drift rate and lexical correlation along edges; compare NCA, GNN and GBDT; locate NYC on those axes by measuring them (for example Moran's I of change on the place graph) | The literature has no controlled study of when locality favours NCAs over trees, so this is a clean contribution | Itself; the deliverable is the phase diagram | None; publishable as a careful negative-plus-boundary result |

Directions 1–3 are product bets. Direction 4 is the one remaining fair test of the cells on real data. Direction 5 turns the work you have already done into something the field lacks. Drop the worker population as a predictive component entirely. If an "organism" framing survives, it belongs in direction 1, as a population of candidate policies, where the cited evidence says evolution actually works. Before you claim any gain from it, benchmark it against the plain algorithm it reduces to: a decayed-score priority queue or a bandit.

## Conclusion

The project's main finding is a clean boundary, not a failure. Learned local dynamics beat trees only when the world is built so that signal travels along edges and changes over time. On one month of real NYC listings, it barely does. That shifts the question from "how do I make the cells win?" to "how much edge-borne, time-varying signal does real listing data contain?", and that question is cheap to measure before any further modelling. If the answer is "little," the value lies in text, labels and policy search. If multi-month data shows real drift and spatial contagion of closures, the cells have a specific and defensible job: online propagation of fresh evidence into a scheduler that is already optimal. In that job they should be judged on budget-matched freshness over long horizons, as Menczer did in 2004, not on single-snapshot AUC.

There is also a reversal of roles. The most valuable NCA results of 2026 use cellular automata as data generators and test-time-compute engines, not as predictors. Your synthetic-world generator is the closest thing in this project to that use, and it may be the most reusable artifact you have built. Turned into a benchmark for "when does graph locality beat boosted trees," it fills a gap in the NCA literature and the knowledge-graph-quality literature at the same time.
